FactEpisode 3 · Equally likely outcomes
When all the outcomes are equally likely:
$P(\text{event}) = \dfrac{\text{number of favourable outcomes}}{\text{total number of outcomes}}$
$P(\text{even})$ is read “the probability of an even number”.
Roll a die: $P(5) = \dfrac{1}{6}$   (one 5, six faces)

Category · ruleEpisode 3 · Equally likely outcomes
Outcomes are equally likely when each one has the same chance of happening.
Only then can we use favourable ÷ total.

We Do 2Episode 3 · Equally likely outcomes
A spinner has $8$ equal sections: $3$ red, $4$ blue and $1$ yellow.
Find $P(\text{red})$.
$3$ red sections out of $8$ equal sections
$P(\text{red}) = \dfrac{3}{8}$
We Do 3Episode 3 · Equally likely outcomes
A bag holds $5$ red, $7$ blue and $3$ green marbles. One marble is taken out at random.
Find $P(\text{blue})$.
Total: $5 + 7 + 3 = 15$
$P(\text{blue}) = \dfrac{7}{15}$

$11$ letters, and B appears $2$ times
$P(\text{B}) = \dfrac{2}{11}$
We Do 5Episode 3 · Equally likely outcomes
A number from $1$ to $20$ is chosen at random.
Find the probability that it is a multiple of $3$ or a multiple of $5$. Give your answer as a percentage.
Multiples of $3$: $3,\ 6,\ 9,\ 12,\ 15,\ 18$
Multiples of $5$: $5,\ 10,\ 20$   ($15$ already counted)
$9$ outcomes, so $P = \dfrac{9}{20} = 45\%$
