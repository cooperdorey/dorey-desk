FactEpisode 8 · Expected frequency
If an experiment is repeated $n$ times and the probability of an event each time is $p$, then
$\text{expected frequency} = np$
Toss a coin $100$ times: expected number of heads $= 100 \times 0.5 = 50$.
The expected frequency is what we expect on average. The actual result can be a little different.

FactEpisode 8 · Expected frequency
Probability in society
Governments, companies and the media use probabilities to make decisions and claims.
• Insurance: companies estimate the expected number of claims to set prices.
• Weather: a $70\%$ chance of rain helps people and councils plan.
• Health: governments use the expected number of cases to plan hospital beds and vaccines.
• Lotteries and games: prizes are set so the company expects to make a profit.
Ask: where did the probability come from, and who benefits from the claim?
We Do 6Episode 8 · Expected frequency
An insurance company has $5000$ car customers. The probability that a customer's car is stolen in a year is $0.004$.
(a) How many thefts should the company expect in a year?
(b) The average claim for a stolen car is $\$15\,000$. Find the expected total cost of claims.
(c) What is the least the company should charge each customer to cover this cost?
(a) $5000 \times 0.004 = 20$ thefts
(b) $20 \times \$15\,000 = \$300\,000$
(c) $\$300\,000 \div 5000 = \$60$ per customer
We Do 7Episode 8 · Expected frequency
An advertisement for a scratch card game says “1 in 8 cards wins a prize!”. Cards cost $\$5$ each, and every prize is $\$20$.
(a) $2000$ cards are sold. How many winning cards would be expected? 1
$2000 \times \dfrac{1}{8} = 250$ cards
(b) Explain why the company expects to make a profit, even though the advertisement makes winning sound likely. 2
Sales: $2000 \times \$5 = \$10\,000$. Expected prizes: $250 \times \$20 = \$5000$.
The company expects to keep $\$5000$. A probability of $0.125$ is unlikely, so most players lose.
