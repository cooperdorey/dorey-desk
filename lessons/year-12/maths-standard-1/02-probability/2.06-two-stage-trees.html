FactEpisode 6 · Two-stage events: tree diagrams
A tree diagram shows a two-stage event as branches.
The first branches show the first stage. From the end of each one, branches show the second stage.
Each path from start to end is one outcome. List the outcomes at the ends.
When every branch at each stage is equally likely, every path is equally likely:
$P = \dfrac{\text{number of paths that fit}}{\text{total number of paths}}$

A family has two children. Each child is equally likely to be a boy (B) or a girl (G).
Find the probability that the family has at least one girl.
“At least one” means one or two: $3$ paths
$P = \dfrac{3}{4}$

A café chooses a lunch special at random: soup or salad, then fish, chicken or pasta.
Find the probability that the special is soup with a main that is not fish.
$2$ of the $6$ paths:   $P = \dfrac{2}{6} = \dfrac{1}{3}$
We Do 4Episode 6 · Two-stage events: tree diagrams
A bag holds a red, a blue and a green counter. Two counters are taken out, one after the other, without replacement.
Find the probability that the red counter is one of the two taken out.
No replacement: only $2$ branches at the second stage, so $6$ paths
$4$ contain R:   $P = \dfrac{4}{6} = \dfrac{2}{3}$
We Do 5Episode 6 · Two-stage events: tree diagrams
The same bag is used, but the first counter is put back (with replacement) before the second is taken out.
Find the probability that both counters are the same colour.
$9$ paths, $3$ the same colour:   $P = \dfrac{3}{9} = \dfrac{1}{3}$
We Do 6Episode 6 · Two-stage events: tree diagrams
A captain and then a vice-captain are chosen at random from Jo, Kim and Lee. The same person cannot be chosen twice.
(a) Draw a tree diagram to show all the possible outcomes. 1
(b) Find the probability that Jo is captain. 1
$2$ of the $6$ paths:   $\dfrac{2}{6} = \dfrac{1}{3}$
(c) Find the probability that Kim is chosen for one of the two roles. 2
Paths with Kim: Jo–Kim, Kim–Jo, Kim–Lee, Lee–Kim
$\dfrac{4}{6} = \dfrac{2}{3}$
