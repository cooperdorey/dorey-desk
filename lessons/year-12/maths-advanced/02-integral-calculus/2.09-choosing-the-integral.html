FactEpisode 9 · Choosing the rule
$\displaystyle\int f'(x)\,e^{f(x)}\,dx=e^{f(x)}+c$
ProofEpisode 9 · Choosing the rule
Proof
$\dfrac{d}{dx}\left[e^{f(x)}\right]=f'(x)\,e^{f(x)}$
So $f'(x)\,e^{f(x)}$ integrates to $e^{f(x)}+c$.
I DoEpisode 9 · Choosing the rule
Find $\displaystyle\int xe^{x^{2}}\,dx$.
$f(x)=x^{2}$, so $f'(x)=2x$
Write $x=\dfrac{1}{2}\times2x$
$\int xe^{x^{2}}\,dx=\dfrac{1}{2}\int2x\,e^{x^{2}}\,dx=\dfrac{1}{2}e^{x^{2}}+c$
We Do 1Episode 9 · Choosing the rule
Find $\displaystyle\int x^{2}e^{x^{3}}\,dx$.
$f(x)=x^{3}$, so $f'(x)=3x^{2}$
$\int x^{2}e^{x^{3}}\,dx=\dfrac{1}{3}\int3x^{2}e^{x^{3}}\,dx=\dfrac{1}{3}e^{x^{3}}+c$
I DoEpisode 9 · Choosing the rule
Find $\displaystyle\int\left(\dfrac{4x}{x^{2}+5}+6e^{-3x}+\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}\right)dx$.
$\dfrac{4x}{x^{2}+5}=2\times\dfrac{2x}{x^{2}+5}$ is $\dfrac{f'}{f}$: $2\ln\left(x^{2}+5\right)$
$6e^{-3x}$ is $e^{ax+b}$: $-2e^{-3x}$
$x^{-2}$ is a power: $-\dfrac{1}{x}$
$=2\ln\left(x^{2}+5\right)-2e^{-3x}-\dfrac{1}{x}+c$
We Do 2Episode 9 · Choosing the rule
Find $\displaystyle\int\left(x^{2}e^{x^{3}}+\dfrac{x^{2}}{x^{3}+1}\right)dx$.
$x^{2}e^{x^{3}}$ is $f'e^{f}$ with $f=x^{3}$: $\dfrac{1}{3}e^{x^{3}}$
$\dfrac{x^{2}}{x^{3}+1}$ is $\dfrac{f'}{f}$ with $f=x^{3}+1$: $\dfrac{1}{3}\ln\left|x^{3}+1\right|$
$=\dfrac{1}{3}e^{x^{3}}+\dfrac{1}{3}\ln\left|x^{3}+1\right|+c$
We Do 3Episode 9 · Choosing the rule
Find a) $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{x^{2}+3x+1}{x}\,dx$
b) $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{e^{2x}+1}{e^{x}}\,dx$
a) Divide each term by $x$.
$\int\left(x+3+\dfrac{1}{x}\right)dx=\dfrac{x^{2}}{2}+3x+\ln|x|+c$
b) $\dfrac{e^{2x}+1}{e^{x}}=e^{x}+e^{-x}$
$\int\left(e^{x}+e^{-x}\right)dx=e^{x}-e^{-x}+c$
We Do 4Episode 9 · Choosing the rule
Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{1}^{2}\left(\dfrac{4x^{3}}{x^{4}+1}+e^{2-x}\right)dx$ in exact form.
$\dfrac{4x^{3}}{x^{4}+1}$ is $\dfrac{f'}{f}$ and $e^{2-x}$ is $e^{ax+b}$.
$\displaystyle\left[\ln\left(x^{4}+1\right)-e^{2-x}\right]_{1}^{2}$
$=\left(\ln17-e^{0}\right)-\left(\ln2-e^{1}\right)$
$=\ln\dfrac{17}{2}+e-1$
We Do 5Episode 9 · Choosing the rule
The gradient of a curve is $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{6x}{x^{2}+1}+e^{-2x}$. The curve passes through $(0,2)$. Find its equation.
$y=3\ln\left(x^{2}+1\right)-\dfrac{1}{2}e^{-2x}+c$
Substitute $(0,2)$: $2=0-\dfrac{1}{2}+c$, so $c=\dfrac{5}{2}$
$y=3\ln\left(x^{2}+1\right)-\dfrac{1}{2}e^{-2x}+\dfrac{5}{2}$
We Do 6Episode 9 · Choosing the rule
Find $\displaystyle\int x\sqrt{x^{2}+1}\,dx$.
$f(x)=x^{2}+1$, so $f'(x)=2x$
$\int x\left(x^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}dx=\dfrac{1}{2}\int2x\left(x^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}dx$
$=\dfrac{1}{2}\times\dfrac{2}{3}\left(x^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}+c=\dfrac{1}{3}\left(x^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}+c$
