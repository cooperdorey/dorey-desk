FactEpisode 4 · Reverse chain rule
The reverse chain rule
$\displaystyle\int f'(x)\,\left[f(x)\right]^{n}dx=\dfrac{1}{n+1}\left[f(x)\right]^{n+1}+c,\quad n\neq-1$

The derivative of the inside function sits outside the bracket.
With $u=f(x)$, this is $\displaystyle\int u^{n}\dfrac{du}{dx}\,dx=\dfrac{1}{n+1}u^{n+1}+c$.
Check by differentiating your answer.

I DoEpisode 4 · Reverse chain rule
Find $\displaystyle\int3x^{2}\left(x^{3}-1\right)^{5}dx$.
The inside is $f(x)=x^{3}-1$, and $f'(x)=3x^{2}$ is outside.
$\int3x^{2}\left(x^{3}-1\right)^{5}dx=\dfrac{\left(x^{3}-1\right)^{6}}{6}+c$
Check: $\dfrac{d}{dx}\dfrac{\left(x^{3}-1\right)^{6}}{6}=\dfrac{6\left(x^{3}-1\right)^{5}\times3x^{2}}{6}=3x^{2}\left(x^{3}-1\right)^{5}$

Find $\displaystyle\int(4x+1)\left(2x^{2}+x\right)^{3}dx$.
The inside is $f(x)=2x^{2}+x$, and $f'(x)=4x+1$ is outside.
$=\dfrac{\left(2x^{2}+x\right)^{4}}{4}+c$
FactEpisode 4 · Reverse chain rule
Off by a constant
If the outside factor is a constant multiple of $f'(x)$, multiply and divide by that constant.
For example, $x$ is $\tfrac{1}{2}$ of $f'(x)=2x$, so $\displaystyle\int x\,[\ldots]\,dx=\tfrac{1}{2}\int2x\,[\ldots]\,dx$.
Only a constant can be fixed this way. A factor of $x$ cannot.
I DoEpisode 4 · Reverse chain rule
Find $\displaystyle\int x\left(3x^{2}+1\right)^{2}dx$.
The inside is $f(x)=3x^{2}+1$, so $f'(x)=6x$.
Write $x=\tfrac{1}{6}\times6x$.
$\int x\left(3x^{2}+1\right)^{2}dx=\tfrac{1}{6}\int6x\left(3x^{2}+1\right)^{2}dx$
$=\tfrac{1}{6}\times\dfrac{\left(3x^{2}+1\right)^{3}}{3}+c$
$=\dfrac{\left(3x^{2}+1\right)^{3}}{18}+c$
We Do 2Episode 4 · Reverse chain rule
Find $\displaystyle\int x^{2}\left(x^{3}+4\right)^{4}dx$.
The inside is $f(x)=x^{3}+4$, so $f'(x)=3x^{2}$.
$=\tfrac{1}{3}\int3x^{2}\left(x^{3}+4\right)^{4}dx$
$=\tfrac{1}{3}\times\dfrac{\left(x^{3}+4\right)^{5}}{5}+c$
$=\dfrac{\left(x^{3}+4\right)^{5}}{15}+c$
I DoEpisode 4 · Reverse chain rule
Find $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{x}{\left(x^{2}+4\right)^{2}}dx$.
Rewrite: $\int x\left(x^{2}+4\right)^{-2}dx$
The inside is $f(x)=x^{2}+4$, so $f'(x)=2x$.
$=\tfrac{1}{2}\int2x\left(x^{2}+4\right)^{-2}dx$
$=\tfrac{1}{2}\times\dfrac{\left(x^{2}+4\right)^{-1}}{-1}+c$
$=-\dfrac{1}{2\left(x^{2}+4\right)}+c$
We Do 3Episode 4 · Reverse chain rule
Find $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^{2}+9}}dx$.
Rewrite: $\int x\left(x^{2}+9\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}dx$
$f'(x)=2x$, so $=\tfrac{1}{2}\int2x\left(x^{2}+9\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}dx$
$=\tfrac{1}{2}\times\dfrac{\left(x^{2}+9\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\frac{1}{2}}+c$
$=\sqrt{x^{2}+9}+c$
We Do 4Episode 4 · Reverse chain rule
Find $\displaystyle\int x\sqrt{x^{2}+1}\,dx$.
Rewrite: $\int x\left(x^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}dx$
$f'(x)=2x$, so $=\tfrac{1}{2}\int2x\left(x^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}dx$
$=\tfrac{1}{2}\times\dfrac{\left(x^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\frac{3}{2}}+c$
$=\tfrac{1}{3}\left(x^{2}+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}+c$
