FactEpisode 4 · The reverse chain rule
The reverse chain rule
$\displaystyle\int f'(x)\,[f(x)]^{n}\,dx=\dfrac{1}{n+1}[f(x)]^{n+1}+C,\quad n\neq-1$
Proof: by the chain rule, $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\dfrac{[f(x)]^{n+1}}{n+1}\right)=\dfrac{(n+1)[f(x)]^{n}\,f'(x)}{n+1}=f'(x)[f(x)]^{n}$.
The integrand must contain $f'(x)$: the derivative of the inside, as a factor.
The inside's derivative is already there, so treat the inside like a single variable.

$f=1+3x^{2},\ f'=6x,\ k=\tfrac{1}{6}$
