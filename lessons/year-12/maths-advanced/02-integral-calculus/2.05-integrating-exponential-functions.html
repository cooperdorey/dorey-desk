FactEpisode 5 · Exponential integrals
Integrals of $e^{x}$ and $e^{ax+b}$
$\displaystyle\int e^{x}\,dx=e^{x}+C\qquad\int e^{ax+b}\,dx=\dfrac{1}{a}\,e^{ax+b}+C$
Check: $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\dfrac{1}{a}e^{ax+b}\right)=\dfrac{1}{a}\times a\,e^{ax+b}=e^{ax+b}$, with $a\neq0$.
The power stays the same. Divide by $a$.

FactEpisode 5 · Exponential integrals
The integral of $a^{x}$
$\displaystyle\int a^{x}\,dx=\dfrac{a^{x}}{\ln a}+C,\quad a\gt 0,\ a\neq1$
Check: $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\dfrac{a^{x}}{\ln a}\right)=\dfrac{a^{x}\ln a}{\ln a}=a^{x}$. Here $\ln a$ is just a number.
For $e^{x}$, $\ln e=1$, so the formula matches the one for $e^{x}$.

FactEpisode 5 · Exponential integrals
The integral of $f'(x)\,e^{f(x)}$
$\displaystyle\int f'(x)\,e^{f(x)}\,dx=e^{f(x)}+C$
Proof: $\dfrac{d}{dx}e^{f(x)}=f'(x)\,e^{f(x)}$ by the chain rule. This is on the reference sheet.

We Do 9Episode 5 · Exponential integrals
Show that the area enclosed by $y=9-3^{x}$ and the coordinate axes is $18-\dfrac{8}{\ln3}$ square units.
$9-3^{x}=0$ when $3^{x}=9$, so $x=2$. The function is positive from $0$ to $2$.
$\text{Area}=\int_{0}^{2}\left(9-3^{x}\right)dx=\left[9x-\dfrac{3^{x}}{\ln3}\right]_{0}^{2}$
$=\left(18-\dfrac{9}{\ln3}\right)-\left(0-\dfrac{1}{\ln3}\right)=18-\dfrac{8}{\ln3}$
