FactEpisode 1 · Definite integrals from geometry
The definite integral
$\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx$

is the area between $y=f(x)$ and the $x$-axis from $x=a$ to $x=b$, where $f(x)\ge0$.
I DoEpisode 1 · Definite integrals from geometry
Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{3}2x\,dx$ using area.
The region under the line is a triangle.
Base $=3$ and height $=f(3)=6$.
Area $=\tfrac{1}{2}\times3\times6=9$
$\int_{0}^{3}2x\,dx=9$
We Do 1Episode 1 · Definite integrals from geometry
Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{1}^{4}2x\,dx$ using area.
The region is a trapezium.
The parallel sides are $f(1)=2$ and $f(4)=8$, and the width is $3$.
Area $=\tfrac{1}{2}\times3\times(2+8)=15$
$\int_{1}^{4}2x\,dx=15$
FactEpisode 1 · Definite integrals from geometry
Signed area
Area above the $x$-axis is positive.
Area below the $x$-axis is negative.
I DoEpisode 1 · Definite integrals from geometry
Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{6}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}x-3\right)dx$ using area.
$f(0)=-3$ and $f(6)=0$, so the whole region is below the axis.
It is a triangle with base $6$ and height $3$, so its area is $\tfrac{1}{2}\times6\times3=9$.
Below the axis counts as negative.
$\int_{0}^{6}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}x-3\right)dx=-9$
We Do 2Episode 1 · Definite integrals from geometry
Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{5}(x-2)\,dx$ using area.
The line crosses the $x$-axis at $x=2$. $f(0)=-2$ and $f(5)=3$.
Below the axis: triangle, base $2$, height $2$, area $2$, so it counts as $-2$.
Above the axis: triangle, base $3$, height $3$, area $4.5$, so it counts as $+4.5$.
$\int_{0}^{5}(x-2)\,dx=-2+4.5=2.5$
We Do 3Episode 1 · Definite integrals from geometry
Show that $\displaystyle\int_{-3}^{3}\sqrt{9-x^{2}}\,dx=\dfrac{9\pi}{2}$.
$y=\sqrt{9-x^{2}}$ is the upper semicircle with centre $(0,0)$ and radius $3$.
Its area is $\tfrac{1}{2}\pi(3)^{2}=\dfrac{9\pi}{2}$.
FactEpisode 1 · Definite integrals from geometry
Odd and even functions
$f$ odd: $\displaystyle\int_{-a}^{a}f(x)\,dx=0$ and $\displaystyle\int_{-a}^{0}f(x)\,dx=-\int_{0}^{a}f(x)\,dx$
$f$ even: $\displaystyle\int_{-a}^{a}f(x)\,dx=2\int_{0}^{a}f(x)\,dx$
I DoEpisode 1 · Definite integrals from geometry
$f$ is continuous and $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{4}f(x)\,dx=5$. Find $\displaystyle\int_{-4}^{4}f(x)\,dx$ if
i) $f$ is even,
ii) $f$ is odd.
i) $f$ is even, so $\int_{-4}^{4}f(x)\,dx=2\times5=10$.
ii) $f$ is odd, so $\int_{-4}^{4}f(x)\,dx=0$.
We Do 4Episode 1 · Definite integrals from geometry
$f$ is an odd function that is continuous for all $x$, and $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}f(x)\,dx=3$.
a) Find $\displaystyle\int_{-2}^{2}f(x)\,dx$.
$f$ is odd, so $\int_{-2}^{2}f(x)\,dx=0$.
b) Find $\displaystyle\int_{-2}^{0}f(x)\,dx$.
$\int_{-2}^{2}f=\int_{-2}^{0}f+\int_{0}^{2}f=0$
So $\int_{-2}^{0}f(x)\,dx=-3$.
We Do 5Episode 1 · Definite integrals from geometry
$f$ is an odd function that is continuous for all $x$, with $f(x)\gt0$ for $0\lt x\lt3$. Also $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f(x)\,dx=2$ and $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{3}f(x)\,dx=7$.
Let $P=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f$, $Q=\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}f$, $R=\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{0}f$ and $S=\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{3}f$.
Which list is in ascending order?
A. $R,\ Q,\ P,\ S$
B. $Q,\ R,\ P,\ S$
C. $R,\ P,\ Q,\ S$
D. $R,\ Q,\ S,\ P$
$P=2$
$R=-P=-2$ (odd)
$Q=0$ (odd)
$S=Q+\int_{1}^{3}f=0+(7-2)=5$
Ascending: $R\,(-2),\ Q\,(0),\ P\,(2),\ S\,(5)$. The answer is A.
