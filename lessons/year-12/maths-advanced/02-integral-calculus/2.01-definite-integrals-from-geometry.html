FactEpisode 1 · Definite integrals from geometry
The definite integral
$\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b} f(x)\,dx$
is the signed area between the graph of $y=f(x)$ and the $x$-axis, from $x=a$ to $x=b$.
Area above the $x$-axis counts as positive. Area below the $x$-axis counts as negative.
A definite integral is a number. It is not always an area, because it can be negative or zero.

FactEpisode 1 · Definite integrals from geometry
Estimating an integral with rectangles
$\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx=\lim_{\Delta x\to0}\sum f(x)\,\Delta x$
Each rectangle has height $f(x)$ and width $\Delta x$, so its area is $f(x)\,\Delta x$.
Adding the rectangles gives an estimate. Thinner rectangles give a better estimate.
If $f(x)$ is a rate of change, then $\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx$ is the total change in the quantity from $a$ to $b$.
The symbol $\int$ is a stretched S, for "sum".

FactEpisode 1 · Definite integrals from geometry
Continuous on an interval A function is continuous on an interval if its graph has no breaks, jumps or gaps there. You could draw it without lifting your pen.
$\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx$ needs $f$ continuous from $a$ to $b$
For example, $y=\dfrac{1}{x}$ breaks at $x=0$. So $\displaystyle\int_{1}^{3}\dfrac{1}{x}\,dx$ is fine, but $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\dfrac{1}{x}\,dx$ is not defined.
Check the interval for breaks before you use any rule.
FactEpisode 1 · Definite integrals from geometry
Constants and sums
$\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}k\,f(x)\,dx=k\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx\qquad\int_{a}^{b}(f(x)+g(x))\,dx=\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx+\int_{a}^{b}g(x)\,dx$
Stack the heights and you stack the areas. A constant $c$ gives a rectangle: $\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}c\,dx=c(b-a)$.
FactEpisode 1 · Definite integrals from geometry
Odd and even functions
Even: $f(-x)=f(x)\ \Longrightarrow\ \displaystyle\int_{-a}^{a}f(x)\,dx=2\int_{0}^{a}f(x)\,dx$
Odd: $f(-x)=-f(x)\ \Longrightarrow\ \displaystyle\int_{-a}^{a}f(x)\,dx=0$
Also $\displaystyle\int_{-a}^{0}f(x)\,dx=\int_{0}^{a}f(x)\,dx$ for even $f$, and $\displaystyle\int_{-a}^{0}f(x)\,dx=-\int_{0}^{a}f(x)\,dx$ for odd $f$.
Even graphs are mirror images across the $y$-axis. Odd graphs turn upside down about the origin, so the signed areas cancel.

We Do 10Episode 1 · Definite integrals from geometry
a) Show that $f(x)=x^{3}$ is an odd function.
$f(-x)=(-x)^{3}=-x^{3}=-f(x)$, so $f$ is odd.
b) Hence show that $\displaystyle\int_{-2}^{2}\left(x^{3}+5\right)dx=20$.
