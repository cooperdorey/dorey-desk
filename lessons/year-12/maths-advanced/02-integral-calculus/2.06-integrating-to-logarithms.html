FactEpisode 6 · Logarithmic integrals
The integral of $\dfrac{1}{x}$
$\displaystyle\int\dfrac{1}{x}\,dx=\ln|x|+C,\quad x\neq0$
Check: $\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln x=\dfrac{1}{x}$ for $x\gt 0$. In HSC working, write $\ln x$ when the interval has $x\gt 0$.
This fills the gap in the power rule: $x^{-1}$ cannot use $\dfrac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}$.
FactEpisode 6 · Logarithmic integrals
The integral of $\dfrac{1}{ax+b}$
$\displaystyle\int\dfrac{1}{ax+b}\,dx=\dfrac{1}{a}\ln|ax+b|+C$
Check: $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\dfrac{1}{a}\ln(ax+b)\right)=\dfrac{1}{a}\times\dfrac{a}{ax+b}=\dfrac{1}{ax+b}$.
Divide by $a$, as with $e^{ax+b}$.

FactEpisode 6 · Logarithmic integrals
The integral of $\dfrac{f'(x)}{f(x)}$
$\displaystyle\int\dfrac{f'(x)}{f(x)}\,dx=\ln|f(x)|+C$
Proof: $\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln f(x)=\dfrac{f'(x)}{f(x)}$ by the chain rule. This is on the reference sheet.
The numerator must be the derivative of the denominator, up to a constant $k$.
