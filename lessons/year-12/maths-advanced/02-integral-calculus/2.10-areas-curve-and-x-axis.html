Warm-upEpisode 10 · Areas to the $x$-axis
a) Find $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}x\,dx$ using the area of a triangle.
$\dfrac{1}{2}\times2\times2=2$
b) Find $\displaystyle\int_{-2}^{0}x\,dx$.
The triangle is below the axis, so $-2$.
c) Find $\displaystyle\int_{-2}^{2}x\,dx$.
$2+(-2)=0$
d) What is the area between $y=x$ and the $x$-axis from $x=-2$ to $x=2$?
Both triangles count as positive area: $2+2=4$
FactEpisode 10 · Areas to the $x$-axis
Above the $x$-axis: $A=\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx$
Below the $x$-axis: $A=-\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx$
I DoEpisode 10 · Areas to the $x$-axis
Find the area enclosed by $y=x^{2}-4$ and the $x$-axis.
The curve meets the axis where $x^{2}-4=0$, so $x=\pm2$.
Between $-2$ and $2$ the curve is below the axis.
$\int_{-2}^{2}\left(x^{2}-4\right)dx=\left[\dfrac{x^{3}}{3}-4x\right]_{-2}^{2}=\left(\dfrac{8}{3}-8\right)-\left(-\dfrac{8}{3}+8\right)=-\dfrac{32}{3}$
$A=\dfrac{32}{3}$ square units
We Do 1Episode 10 · Areas to the $x$-axis
Find the area enclosed by $y=9-x^{2}$ and the $x$-axis.
$9-x^{2}=0$ gives $x=\pm3$. The curve is above the axis between them.
$\int_{-3}^{3}\left(9-x^{2}\right)dx=\left[9x-\dfrac{x^{3}}{3}\right]_{-3}^{3}=(27-9)-(-27+9)=36$
$A=36$ square units
FactEpisode 10 · Areas to the $x$-axis
If the curve crosses the $x$-axis at $x=c$ inside $[a,b]$:
$A=\left|\displaystyle\int_{a}^{c}f(x)\,dx\right|+\left|\displaystyle\int_{c}^{b}f(x)\,dx\right|$
I DoEpisode 10 · Areas to the $x$-axis
Find the total area between $y=x(x-2)(x-4)$ and the $x$-axis from $x=0$ to $x=4$.
$y=x^{3}-6x^{2}+8x$ crosses the axis at $x=0,2,4$.
$F(x)=\dfrac{x^{4}}{4}-2x^{3}+4x^{2}$
$\int_{0}^{2}y\,dx=F(2)-F(0)=4$ (above the axis)
$\int_{2}^{4}y\,dx=F(4)-F(2)=0-4=-4$ (below the axis)
$A=4+4=8$ square units, but $\int_{0}^{4}y\,dx=0$.
We Do 2Episode 10 · Areas to the $x$-axis
The graph of $y=x(x-3)$ is shown for $0\leq x\leq5$. Find the total area between the curve and the $x$-axis from $x=0$ to $x=5$.
Crosses the axis at $x=0$ and $x=3$.
$F(x)=\dfrac{x^{3}}{3}-\dfrac{3x^{2}}{2}$
$\int_{0}^{3}y\,dx=F(3)-F(0)=-\dfrac{9}{2}$, so this area is $\dfrac{9}{2}$
$\int_{3}^{5}y\,dx=F(5)-F(3)=\dfrac{25}{6}+\dfrac{9}{2}=\dfrac{26}{3}$
$A=\dfrac{9}{2}+\dfrac{26}{3}=\dfrac{79}{6}$ square units
We Do 3Episode 10 · Areas to the $x$-axis
Find the total area between $y=e^{x}-2$ and the $x$-axis from $x=0$ to $x=2$.
$e^{x}-2=0$ at $x=\ln2$, which is inside $[0,2]$.
$F(x)=e^{x}-2x$
$\int_{0}^{\ln2}\left(e^{x}-2\right)dx=F(\ln2)-F(0)=(2-2\ln2)-1=1-2\ln2$, which is negative, so this area is $2\ln2-1$
$\int_{\ln2}^{2}\left(e^{x}-2\right)dx=F(2)-F(\ln2)=\left(e^{2}-4\right)-(2-2\ln2)=e^{2}-6+2\ln2$
$A=(2\ln2-1)+\left(e^{2}-6+2\ln2\right)=e^{2}-7+4\ln2$ square units
We Do 4Episode 10 · Areas to the $x$-axis
Find the area between $y=\dfrac{4}{2x+1}$, the $x$-axis and the lines $x=0$ and $x=2$.
The curve is above the axis for $0\leq x\leq2$.
$\int_{0}^{2}\dfrac{4}{2x+1}\,dx=[2\ln(2x+1)]_{0}^{2}=2\ln5-2\ln1$
$A=2\ln5$ square units
We Do 5Episode 10 · Areas to the $x$-axis
The regions bounded by the $x$-axis and the graph of $y=x\sin x$ for $x\geq0$ lie between $x=(n-1)\pi$ and $x=n\pi$ for $n=1,2,3,\dots$ Let $A_{n}=\displaystyle\int_{(n-1)\pi}^{n\pi}x\sin x\,dx$. It can be shown that $|A_{n}|=(2n-1)\pi$. Find the exact total area of the regions bounded by the curve and the $x$-axis between $x=0$ and $x=2025\pi$.
$\sin x$ changes sign at every multiple of $\pi$, so each $A_{n}$ is a separate region.
Total area $=|A_{1}|+|A_{2}|+\dots+|A_{2025}|=\pi(1+3+5+\dots+4049)$
The sum of the first $2025$ odd numbers is $2025^{2}$.
Total area $=2025^{2}\pi=4\,100\,625\pi$ square units
