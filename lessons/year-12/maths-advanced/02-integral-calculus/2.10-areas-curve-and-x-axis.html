FactEpisode 10 · Areas to the $x$-axis
Area above the $x$-axis
If $f(x)\geq0$ for $a\leq x\leq b$, the area between the curve and the $x$-axis is
$\displaystyle A=\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx$
The definite integral adds up thin rectangles of height $f(x)$, which are positive above the axis.
Area is never negative.
FactEpisode 10 · Areas to the $x$-axis
Area below the $x$-axis
If $f(x)\leq0$ for $a\leq x\leq b$, then $\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx\leq0$ and the area is
$\displaystyle A=-\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx=\left|\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx\right|$
Heights below the axis are negative in the integral, so change the sign to get a positive area.
Check the sign before you answer.

I DoEpisode 10 · Areas to the $x$-axis
Find the exact area enclosed by the curve $y=x^{2}-4$ and the $x$-axis.
The curve meets the axis where $x^{2}-4=0$, so $x=\pm2$.
Between $-2$ and $2$ the curve is below the axis.
$\int_{-2}^{2}(x^{2}-4)dx=\left[\dfrac{x^{3}}{3}-4x\right]_{-2}^{2}=\left(\tfrac{8}{3}-8\right)-\left(-\tfrac{8}{3}+8\right)=-\tfrac{32}{3}$
$\text{Area}=\tfrac{32}{3}$ square units

$\int_{-3}^{3}(9-x^{2})dx=\left[9x-\tfrac{x^{3}}{3}\right]_{-3}^{3}=18-(-18)=36$
Area $=36$ square units

Category · ruleEpisode 10 · Areas to the $x$-axis
If the curve crosses the axis
1. Find the $x$-intercepts inside $[a,b]$.
2. Integrate each section separately.
3. Make every section positive, then add.
I DoEpisode 10 · Areas to the $x$-axis
Find the total area between $y=x(x-2)(x-4)$ and the $x$-axis from $x=0$ to $x=4$.
$y=x^{3}-6x^{2}+8x$ crosses the axis at $x=0,2,4$.
$F(x)=\dfrac{x^{4}}{4}-2x^{3}+4x^{2}$
$\int_{0}^{2}y\,dx=F(2)-F(0)=4$ (above the axis)
$\int_{2}^{4}y\,dx=F(4)-F(2)=0-4=-4$ (below the axis)
Area $=4+4=8$ square units
We Do 3Episode 10 · Areas to the $x$-axis
The graph of $y=x(x-3)$ is shown for $0\leq x\leq 5$. Find the total area between the curve and the $x$-axis from $x=0$ to $x=5$.
Crosses the axis at $x=0$ and $x=3$.
$F(x)=\dfrac{x^{3}}{3}-\dfrac{3x^{2}}{2}$
$F(3)-F(0)=9-13.5=-4.5$, so this area is $4.5$
$F(5)-F(3)=\left(\tfrac{125}{3}-\tfrac{75}{2}\right)-(-4.5)=\tfrac{25}{6}+\tfrac{9}{2}=\tfrac{26}{3}$
Total area $=\tfrac{9}{2}+\tfrac{26}{3}=\tfrac{79}{6}$ square units
We Do 4Episode 10 · Areas to the $x$-axis
Find the total area between $y=2\cos x-1$ and the $x$-axis from $x=-\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ to $x=\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
$2\cos x-1=0$ at $x=\pm\tfrac{\pi}{3}$ and the curve is above the axis between.
$[2\sin x-x]_{-\pi/3}^{\pi/3}=\left(\sqrt{3}-\tfrac{\pi}{3}\right)-\left(-\sqrt{3}+\tfrac{\pi}{3}\right)=2\sqrt{3}-\tfrac{2\pi}{3}$ square units
We Do 5Episode 10 · Areas to the $x$-axis
Find the total area between $y=e^{x}-2$, the $x$-axis and the lines $x=0$ and $x=\ln 4$ in exact form.
$e^{x}-2=0$ at $x=\ln2$.
$\int_{0}^{\ln2}(e^{x}-2)dx=[e^{x}-2x]_{0}^{\ln2}=(2-2\ln2)-1=1-2\ln2$ (negative, as $2\ln2\gt 1$)
$\int_{\ln2}^{\ln4}(e^{x}-2)dx=(4-2\ln4)-(2-2\ln2)=2-2\ln2$
Total $=(2\ln2-1)+(2-2\ln2)=1$ square unit
We Do 6Episode 10 · Areas to the $x$-axis
The region is under $y=2x$ for $0\leq x\leq1$ and under $y=\dfrac{2}{x}$ for $1\leq x\leq3$, above the $x$-axis. Find its exact area.
$\int_{0}^{1}2x\,dx=1$
$\int_{1}^{3}\dfrac{2}{x}dx=[2\ln x]_{1}^{3}=2\ln3$
Area $=1+2\ln3$ square units

We Do 7Episode 10 · Areas to the $x$-axis
The area between $y=\dfrac{4}{2x+1}$, the $x$-axis, $x=0$ and $x=k$ is $2\ln 5$ square units. Find $k$, where $k\gt 0$.

We Do 8Episode 10 · Areas to the $x$-axis
Show that the area enclosed by $y=8-2^{x}$ and the coordinate axes is $24-\dfrac{7}{\ln 2}$ square units.
$8-2^{x}=0$ when $x=3$.
$\int_{0}^{3}\left(8-2^{x}\right)dx=\left[8x-\dfrac{2^{x}}{\ln2}\right]_{0}^{3}=\left(24-\dfrac{8}{\ln2}\right)-\left(0-\dfrac{1}{\ln2}\right)=24-\dfrac{7}{\ln2}$
Modelled on HSC 2021 Q28(a).
We Do 9Episode 10 · Areas to the $x$-axis
Explain why $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sin x\,dx=0$ but the area between $y=\sin x$ and the $x$-axis from $0$ to $2\pi$ is $4$.
$\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin x\,dx=2$ (above the axis) and $\int_{\pi}^{2\pi}\sin x\,dx=-2$ (below).
The integral adds $2+(-2)=0$. Area adds $2+2=4$.
