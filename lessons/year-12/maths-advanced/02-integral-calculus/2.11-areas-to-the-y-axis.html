FactEpisode 11 · Areas to the $y$-axis
Area between a curve and the $y$-axis
For a curve $x=g(y)\geq0$, the area between the curve, the $y$-axis and the lines $y=c$ and $y=d$ is
$\displaystyle A=\int_{c}^{d}x\,dy=\int_{c}^{d}g(y)\,dy$
Use horizontal strips of width $x$ and thickness $dy$. The limits are $y$-values.
Write the curve with $x$ as a function of $y$ first.

We Do 5Episode 11 · Areas to the $y$-axis
The region is bounded by $y=e^{x}$, the $y$-axis and the line $y=e^{2}$. Find its exact area by integrating with respect to $x$.
The curve meets $y=e^{2}$ at $x=2$. The region lies between the line (above) and the curve (below).
$\int_{0}^{2}\left(e^{2}-e^{x}\right)dx=[e^{2}x-e^{x}]_{0}^{2}=\left(2e^{2}-e^{2}\right)-(0-1)=e^{2}+1$ square units
We Do 6Episode 11 · Areas to the $y$-axis
Show that the area between $y=\ln x$, the $x$-axis and $x=e$ is $1$ square unit, by using the $y$-axis. (Hint: the rectangle $0\leq x\leq e$, $0\leq y\leq1$ has area $e$.)
The region left of $x=e^{y}$ (between the $y$-axis and the curve) for $0\leq y\leq1$ has area $\int_{0}^{1}e^{y}dy=e-1$.
The wanted region is the rectangle minus this part: $e-(e-1)=1$ square unit.
We Do 7Episode 11 · Areas to the $y$-axis
The curve $y=2^{x}$ meets $y=1$ at $x=0$ and $y=8$ at $x=3$. Find the exact area between the curve, the $y$-axis and the lines $y=1$ and $y=8$.
Rectangle $0\leq x\leq3$, $1\leq y\leq8$ has area $3\times7=21$.
Part below the curve: $\int_{0}^{3}\left(2^{x}-1\right)dx=\left[\dfrac{2^{x}}{\ln2}-x\right]_{0}^{3}=\dfrac{7}{\ln2}-3$
Required area $=21-\left(\dfrac{7}{\ln2}-3\right)=24-\dfrac{7}{\ln2}$ square units
We Do 8Episode 11 · Areas to the $y$-axis
Explain why your answer to the last question equals the area enclosed by $y=8-2^{x}$ and the coordinate axes.
Region under $y=8-2^{x}$ ($0\leq x\leq3$) is the rectangle $0\leq x\leq3$, $0\leq y\leq8$ (area $24$) minus the area under $y=2^{x}$ (which is $\dfrac{7}{\ln2}$), giving $24-\dfrac{7}{\ln2}$.
The region in the last question is the rectangle $3\times7=21$ minus the part below $2^{x}$ and above $y=1$, which is $21-\left(\dfrac{7}{\ln2}-3\right)$. Both simplify to $24-\dfrac{7}{\ln2}$.
