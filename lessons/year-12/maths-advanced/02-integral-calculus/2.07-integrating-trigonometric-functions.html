Differentiate each function.
a) $y=\sin x$

b) $y=\cos x$

c) $y=\tan x$
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\sec^{2}x$

$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=3\cos3x$

Divide (d) by $3$.

FactEpisode 7 · Trigonometric functions
Integrating $\sin x$, $\cos x$ and $\sec^{2}x$
$\displaystyle\int\sin x\,dx=-\cos x+c$
$\displaystyle\int\cos x\,dx=\sin x+c$
$\displaystyle\int\sec^{2}x\,dx=\tan x+c$

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin\dfrac{x}{2}\,dx$.
$a=\tfrac{1}{2}$, so divide by $\tfrac{1}{2}$.
$\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin\dfrac{x}{2}\,dx=\left[-2\cos\dfrac{x}{2}\right]_{0}^{\pi}$
$=-2\cos\dfrac{\pi}{2}-\left(-2\cos0\right)$
$=0+2=2$
FactEpisode 7 · Trigonometric functions
Integrating $f'(x)\sin f(x)$
$\displaystyle\int f'(x)\sin f(x)\,dx=-\cos f(x)+c$
$\displaystyle\int f'(x)\cos f(x)\,dx=\sin f(x)+c$
$\displaystyle\int f'(x)\sec^{2}f(x)\,dx=\tan f(x)+c$

We Do 4Episode 7 · Trigonometric functions
A camera films the motion of a swing. Let $x(t)$ be the horizontal distance, in metres, from the camera to the seat of the swing at $t$ seconds. The seat is released from rest at a horizontal distance of $11.2$ m from the camera.
The rate of change of $x$ is modelled by $\dfrac{dx}{dt}=-1.5\pi\sin\dfrac{5\pi}{4}t$.
Find an expression for $x(t)$.
$x(t)=-1.5\pi\times\left(-\dfrac{4}{5\pi}\right)\cos\dfrac{5\pi t}{4}+c=\dfrac{6}{5}\cos\dfrac{5\pi t}{4}+c$
$x(0)=11.2$: $\dfrac{6}{5}+c=11.2$, so $c=10$.
$x(t)=10+\dfrac{6}{5}\cos\dfrac{5\pi t}{4}$

We Do 5Episode 7 · Trigonometric functions
a) Find $\displaystyle\int\cos2x\,dx$.
b) It is given that $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{f'(x)}{f(x)}\,dx=\int\cos2x\,dx$, where $f(0)=1$ and $f(x)\gt 0$ for all real $x$. Find $f(x)$.
a) $\int\cos2x\,dx=\dfrac{1}{2}\sin2x+c$
b) $\int\dfrac{f'(x)}{f(x)}\,dx=\ln f(x)+c$ since $f(x)\gt 0$.
So $\ln f(x)=\dfrac{1}{2}\sin2x+c$.
$f(0)=1$: $\ln1=\dfrac{1}{2}\sin0+c$, so $c=0$.
$f(x)=e^{\frac{1}{2}\sin2x}$
