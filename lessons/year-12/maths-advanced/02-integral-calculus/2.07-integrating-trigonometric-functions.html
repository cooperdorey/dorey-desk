FactEpisode 7 · Trigonometric integrals
Integrals of $\cos x$, $\sin x$ and $\sec^{2}x$
$\displaystyle\int\cos x\,dx=\sin x+C\qquad\int\sin x\,dx=-\cos x+C\qquad\int\sec^{2}x\,dx=\tan x+C$
Check each by differentiating. $\dfrac{d}{dx}(-\cos x)=\sin x$, so the minus sign is needed for $\sin x$.
Angles are in radians.
FactEpisode 7 · Trigonometric integrals
Integrals with $ax+b$
$\displaystyle\int\cos(ax+b)\,dx=\dfrac{1}{a}\sin(ax+b)+C$
$\displaystyle\int\sin(ax+b)\,dx=-\dfrac{1}{a}\cos(ax+b)+C$
$\displaystyle\int\sec^{2}(ax+b)\,dx=\dfrac{1}{a}\tan(ax+b)+C$
Check: $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\dfrac{1}{a}\sin(ax+b)\right)=\dfrac{1}{a}\times a\cos(ax+b)$.
Divide by $a$. Keep the sign from the basic integral.

FactEpisode 7 · Trigonometric integrals
Integrating $\tan x$ and a trig quotient
$\displaystyle\int\dfrac{f'(x)}{f(x)}\,dx=\ln|f(x)|+C$ applies when $f$ is a trig function
For example $\dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x}$ has $f=\sin x$ and $f'=\cos x$, so $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x}dx=\ln|\sin x|+C$.
Spot a numerator that is the derivative of the denominator.
We Do 8Episode 7 · Trigonometric integrals
Find $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x}\,dx$ and hence evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{\pi/6}^{\pi/2}\dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x}\,dx$.
$f=\sin x,\ f'=\cos x$
$\int\dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x}dx=\ln|\sin x|+C$
$[\ln\sin x]_{\pi/6}^{\pi/2}=\ln1-\ln\tfrac{1}{2}=\ln2$
We Do 9Episode 7 · Trigonometric integrals
Show that $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/3}\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}\,dx=\ln2$.
$f=\cos x,\ f'=-\sin x$, so $\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}=-\dfrac{f'}{f}$
$[-\ln\cos x]_{0}^{\pi/3}=-\ln\tfrac{1}{2}+\ln1=\ln2$
We Do 10Episode 7 · Trigonometric integrals
Prove that $\displaystyle\int\sin(ax+b)\,dx=-\dfrac{1}{a}\cos(ax+b)+C$ for $a\neq0$, then find $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/3}\sin\left(3x+\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)dx$.
$\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(-\dfrac{1}{a}\cos(ax+b)\right)=-\dfrac{1}{a}\times\left(-a\sin(ax+b)\right)=\sin(ax+b)$ ✓
$\left[-\tfrac{1}{3}\cos\left(3x+\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)\right]_{0}^{\pi/3}=-\tfrac{1}{3}\cos\tfrac{3\pi}{2}+\tfrac{1}{3}\cos\tfrac{\pi}{2}=0$
