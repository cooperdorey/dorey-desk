FactEpisode 2 · Primitive functions
Primitive functions
$F(x)$ is a primitive of $f(x)$ if $F'(x)=f(x)$
$x^{3}$, $x^{3}+5$ and $x^{3}-2$ all have derivative $3x^{2}$, so the general primitive of $3x^{2}$ is $x^{3}+C$.
If a function has derivative $0$ everywhere, it is a constant. So any two primitives of $f$ differ by a constant.
Finding a primitive reverses differentiation. Always add $+C$.
FactEpisode 2 · Primitive functions
The primitive of $x^{n}$
$x^{n}\ \longrightarrow\ \dfrac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}+C,\quad n\neq-1$
Proof: $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\dfrac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}+C\right)=\dfrac{(n+1)x^{n}}{n+1}=x^{n}$.
So $kx^{n}\longrightarrow\dfrac{kx^{n+1}}{n+1}+C$ and a constant $k\longrightarrow kx+C$.
Add one to the power, then divide by the new power.
FactEpisode 2 · Primitive functions
Notation and rules
$\displaystyle\int f(x)\,dx=F(x)+C$
Read as \"the integral of $f(x)$ with respect to $x$\".
$\displaystyle\int kf(x)\,dx=k\int f(x)\,dx\qquad \int(f(x)+g(x))\,dx=\int f(x)\,dx+\int g(x)\,dx$
Integrate term by term, with one $+C$ at the end.

FactEpisode 2 · Primitive functions
The primitive of $(ax+b)^{n}$
$\displaystyle\int(ax+b)^{n}\,dx=\dfrac{(ax+b)^{n+1}}{a(n+1)}+C,\quad n\neq-1,\ a\neq0$
Check by differentiating: the chain rule brings out a factor $a$, so we divide by $a$.
Add one to the power, then divide by the new power and by $a$.

We Do 12Episode 2 · Primitive functions
Prove by differentiation that:
a) $F(x)=x^{3}+\dfrac{1}{x}$ is a primitive of $f(x)=3x^{2}-\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$.
$F(x)=x^{3}+x^{-1}$
$F'(x)=3x^{2}-x^{-2}=3x^{2}-\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}=f(x)$ ✓
b) If $F$ and $G$ are primitives of $f$ and $g$, and $k$ is a constant, then $F+G$ is a primitive of $f+g$ and $kF$ is a primitive of $kf$.
$\dfrac{d}{dx}(F+G)=F'+G'=f+g$
$\dfrac{d}{dx}(kF)=kF'=kf$
