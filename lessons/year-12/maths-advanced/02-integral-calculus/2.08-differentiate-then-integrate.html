2.08 – Differentiate, Then Integrate
Warm-upEpisode 8 · Differentiate, then integrate
a) Differentiate $y=x^{3}+x$.
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=3x^{2}+1$
b) Use (a) to write down $\displaystyle\int\left(3x^{2}+1\right)dx$.
A primitive of $3x^{2}+1$ is $x^{3}+x$.
$\int\left(3x^{2}+1\right)dx=x^{3}+x+c$
c) Differentiate $y=\sin2x$.
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=2\cos2x$
d) Use (c) to write down $\displaystyle\int\cos2x\,dx$.
Divide (c) by $2$.
$\int\cos2x\,dx=\dfrac{1}{2}\sin2x+c$
FactEpisode 8 · Differentiate, then integrate
Hence
$\dfrac{d}{dx}F(x)=f(x)\ \Longrightarrow\ \displaystyle\int f(x)\,dx=F(x)+c$
I DoEpisode 8 · Differentiate, then integrate
a) Differentiate $xe^{2x}$.
b) Hence find $\displaystyle\int(1+2x)e^{2x}\,dx$.
a) $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(xe^{2x}\right)=1\times e^{2x}+x\times2e^{2x}=(1+2x)e^{2x}$
b) The derivative in (a) is the integrand.
$\int(1+2x)e^{2x}\,dx=xe^{2x}+c$
We Do 1Episode 8 · Differentiate, then integrate
a) Differentiate $y=\left(x^{2}+1\right)^{4}$.
b) Hence, or otherwise, find $\displaystyle\int x\left(x^{2}+1\right)^{3}dx$.
a) $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=4\left(x^{2}+1\right)^{3}\times2x=8x\left(x^{2}+1\right)^{3}$
b) Divide (a) by $8$.
$\int x\left(x^{2}+1\right)^{3}dx=\dfrac{\left(x^{2}+1\right)^{4}}{8}+c$

FactEpisode 8 · Differentiate, then integrate
Rearranging
If $F'(x)=g(x)+h(x)$, then
$\displaystyle\int h(x)\,dx=F(x)-\int g(x)\,dx$
I DoEpisode 8 · Differentiate, then integrate
a) Differentiate $x\sin x$.
b) Hence find $\displaystyle\int x\cos x\,dx$.
a) $\dfrac{d}{dx}(x\sin x)=\sin x+x\cos x$
b) Integrate both sides of (a).
$x\sin x=\int\sin x\,dx+\int x\cos x\,dx$
$\int x\cos x\,dx=x\sin x-\int\sin x\,dx$
$=x\sin x+\cos x+c$
We Do 2Episode 8 · Differentiate, then integrate
a) Differentiate $xe^{x}$.
b) Hence find $\displaystyle\int xe^{x}\,dx$.
a) $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(xe^{x}\right)=e^{x}+xe^{x}$
b) $xe^{x}=\int e^{x}\,dx+\int xe^{x}\,dx$
$\int xe^{x}\,dx=xe^{x}-\int e^{x}\,dx$
$=xe^{x}-e^{x}+c$
We Do 3Episode 8 · Differentiate, then integrate
a) Differentiate $y=\tan x-x$. Use $1+\tan^{2}x=\sec^{2}x$ to simplify.
b) Hence find $\displaystyle\int\tan^{2}x\,dx$.
a) $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\sec^{2}x-1=\tan^{2}x$
b) The derivative in (a) is the integrand.
$\int\tan^{2}x\,dx=\tan x-x+c$
We Do 4Episode 8 · Differentiate, then integrate
a) Show that $\dfrac{d}{dx}(\sin x-x\cos x)=x\sin x$.
b) Hence find the value of $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2025\pi}x\sin x\,dx$.
a) $\dfrac{d}{dx}(\sin x-x\cos x)=\cos x-\left(\cos x-x\sin x\right)=x\sin x$
b) $\int_{0}^{2025\pi}x\sin x\,dx=\left[\sin x-x\cos x\right]_{0}^{2025\pi}$
$=\left(\sin2025\pi-2025\pi\cos2025\pi\right)-\left(0-0\right)$
$=0-2025\pi\times(-1)=2025\pi$

We Do 5Episode 8 · Differentiate, then integrate
a) Find the derivative of $x^{2}\tan x$.
b) Hence, find $\displaystyle\int(x\tan x+1)^{2}\,dx$.
a) $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(x^{2}\tan x\right)=2x\tan x+x^{2}\sec^{2}x$
b) $(x\tan x+1)^{2}=x^{2}\tan^{2}x+2x\tan x+1$
$=x^{2}\left(\sec^{2}x-1\right)+2x\tan x+1$
$=\left(x^{2}\sec^{2}x+2x\tan x\right)-x^{2}+1$
The bracket is the derivative from (a).
$\int(x\tan x+1)^{2}\,dx=x^{2}\tan x-\dfrac{x^{3}}{3}+x+c$
