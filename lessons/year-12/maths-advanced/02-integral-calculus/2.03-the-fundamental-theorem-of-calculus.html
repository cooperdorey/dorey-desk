Warm-upEpisode 3 · Fundamental Theorem
Use the graph of $y=2x$.
a) Find the area under $y=2x$ from $x=0$ to $x=4$.
Triangle with base $4$ and height $8$.
Area $=\tfrac{1}{2}\times4\times8=16$
b) Find the area under $y=2x$ from $x=0$ to $x=1$.
Triangle with base $1$ and height $2$.
Area $=\tfrac{1}{2}\times1\times2=1$
c) Use (a) and (b) to find the area from $x=1$ to $x=4$.
$16-1=15$
d) Write the area from $x=0$ to $x=t$ as a function of $t$.
Base $t$ and height $2t$.
Area $=\tfrac{1}{2}\times t\times2t=t^{2}$
e) Compare your answers to (a) and (b) with $t^{2}$.
$16=4^{2}$ and $1=1^{2}$.
$t^{2}$ is a primitive of $2t$, and the area from $1$ to $4$ is $4^{2}-1^{2}$.
FactEpisode 3 · Fundamental Theorem
The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus
$\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx=\left[F(x)\right]_{a}^{b}=F(b)-F(a)$

where $F(x)$ is any primitive of $f(x)$, and $f$ is continuous on $a\le x\le b$.
I DoEpisode 3 · Fundamental Theorem
Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{1}^{4}2x\,dx$.
A primitive of $2x$ is $x^{2}$.
$\int_{1}^{4}2x\,dx=\left[x^{2}\right]_{1}^{4}=4^{2}-1^{2}=15$

$=\left(\dfrac{8}{3}-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)-\left(\dfrac{1}{3}-1\right)$

FactEpisode 3 · Fundamental Theorem
Rules for limits
$\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}f\,dx+\int_{b}^{c}f\,dx=\int_{a}^{c}f\,dx$ for $a\le b\le c$
$\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}f\,dx=-\int_{b}^{a}f\,dx\qquad\int_{a}^{a}f\,dx=0$
$\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}kf\,dx=k\int_{a}^{b}f\,dx\qquad\int_{a}^{b}(f+g)\,dx=\int_{a}^{b}f\,dx+\int_{a}^{b}g\,dx$
ProofEpisode 3 · Fundamental Theorem
Rules for limits
Let $F$ be a primitive of $f$.
$\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}f\,dx+\int_{b}^{c}f\,dx=\left[F(b)-F(a)\right]+\left[F(c)-F(b)\right]$
$=F(c)-F(a)=\displaystyle\int_{a}^{c}f\,dx$
$\displaystyle\int_{b}^{a}f\,dx=F(a)-F(b)=-\left[F(b)-F(a)\right]$
$=-\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}f\,dx$
I DoEpisode 3 · Fundamental Theorem
$\displaystyle\int_{1}^{4}f(x)\,dx=6$ and $\displaystyle\int_{4}^{7}f(x)\,dx=-2$. Find
i) $\displaystyle\int_{1}^{7}f(x)\,dx$,
ii) $\displaystyle\int_{4}^{1}f(x)\,dx$.
i) $\int_{1}^{7}f=\int_{1}^{4}f+\int_{4}^{7}f=6+(-2)=4$
ii) Reversing the limits changes the sign.
$\int_{4}^{1}f=-\int_{1}^{4}f=-6$

FactEpisode 3 · Fundamental Theorem
Variable upper limit
$A(x)=\displaystyle\int_{a}^{x}f(t)\,dt$

$A(x)$ is a primitive of $f(x)$, so $A'(x)=f(x)$.
I DoEpisode 3 · Fundamental Theorem
Find $A(x)=\displaystyle\int_{1}^{x}(2t+3)\,dt$, then find $A'(x)$.
$A(x)=\left[t^{2}+3t\right]_{1}^{x}=\left(x^{2}+3x\right)-4=x^{2}+3x-4$
$A'(x)=2x+3$
This is $f(x)$, the function inside the integral.
We Do 3Episode 3 · Fundamental Theorem
Find $A(x)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\left(t^{2}-4\right)dt$, then find $A'(x)$.
$A(x)=\left[\dfrac{t^{3}}{3}-4t\right]_{0}^{x}=\dfrac{x^{3}}{3}-4x$
$A'(x)=x^{2}-4$
We Do 4Episode 3 · Fundamental Theorem
$g(x)=\displaystyle\int_{x}^{3}\left(t^{2}+1\right)dt$. Let $F(t)$ be a primitive of $t^{2}+1$. Do not find $F$.
a) Write $g(x)$ in terms of $F$.
$g(x)=F(3)-F(x)$
b) Find $g'(x)$.
$F(3)$ is a constant, and $F'(x)=x^{2}+1$.
$g'(x)=-\left(x^{2}+1\right)$
We Do 5Episode 3 · Fundamental Theorem
Let $g(x)=\displaystyle\int_{x^{3}}^{1}\tan(t)\,dt$, and let $F(t)$ be a primitive of $\tan(t)$, where $0\le x\le1$. Do not find $F$.
a) Find an expression for $g(x)$ in terms of $F$.
$g(x)=F(1)-F\left(x^{3}\right)$
b) Hence find $g'(x)$.
$F(1)$ is a constant.
By the chain rule, $\dfrac{d}{dx}F\left(x^{3}\right)=F'\left(x^{3}\right)\times3x^{2}=3x^{2}\tan\left(x^{3}\right)$
$g'(x)=-3x^{2}\tan\left(x^{3}\right)$
