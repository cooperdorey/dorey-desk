FactEpisode 12 · Numerical estimates of area
One trapezium under a curve
$\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx\approx\dfrac{b-a}{2}[f(a)+f(b)]$
Join the points $(a,f(a))$ and $(b,f(b))$ with a straight line. The shape under it is a trapezium with parallel sides $f(a)$ and $f(b)$ and height $b-a$.
This is an estimate, not the exact area.

FactEpisode 12 · Numerical estimates of area
The trapezoidal rule with $n$ strips
$\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx\approx\dfrac{h}{2}[f(x_{0})+2(f(x_{1})+\cdots+f(x_{n-1}))+f(x_{n})]$
where $h=\dfrac{b-a}{n}$ and $x_{k}=a+kh$
Each inner value is used in two neighbouring trapezia, so it is doubled. The ends are used once. This formula is on the reference sheet.
'Two applications' means $n=2$ strips.

For $f(x)=e^{x^{2}}$, show that $f''(x)\gt 0$ for all $x$, and so state whether the estimate $1.491$ from the I Do is an over- or under-estimate.
$f'(x)=2xe^{x^{2}}$
$f''(x)=2e^{x^{2}}+4x^{2}e^{x^{2}}=(2+4x^{2})e^{x^{2}}\gt 0$ for all $x$.
The curve is concave up, so the trapezoidal estimate is an over-estimate.

FactEpisode 12 · Numerical estimates of area
Rectangles give bounds
For an increasing function: left-end rectangles give an under-estimate, right-end rectangles give an over-estimate.
For a decreasing function the two swap.
With $n$ strips of width $h$: left sum $=h[f(x_{0})+\cdots+f(x_{n-1})]$ and right sum $=h[f(x_{1})+\cdots+f(x_{n})]$.
The exact area lies between the two sums.
