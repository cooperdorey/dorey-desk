FactEpisode 13 · Comparing sums and integrals
Rectangles and a decreasing curve
Let $f$ be positive and decreasing. Rectangles of width $1$ give
$\displaystyle f(1)+f(2)+\cdots+f(n)\;\lt \;\int_{0}^{n}f(x)\,dx\;\lt \;f(0)+f(1)+\cdots+f(n-1)$
Right-end rectangles sit under the curve. Left-end rectangles stick out above it.
The integral lies between two sums.

FactEpisode 13 · Comparing sums and integrals
Limiting sum of rectangles under $y=b^{-x}$
Rectangles of width $1$ and heights $f(0),f(1),f(2),\ldots$ under $y=b^{-x}$ ($b\gt 1$) have areas $1,\ \dfrac{1}{b},\ \dfrac{1}{b^{2}},\ldots$
Limiting sum $=\dfrac{1}{1-\frac{1}{b}}=\dfrac{b}{b-1}$
This is a geometric series with $a=1$ and $r=\dfrac{1}{b}$, and $|r|\lt 1$.
All the left-end rectangles together are bigger than any integral from $0$.

I DoEpisode 13 · Comparing sums and integrals
The rectangles of width $1$ and height $y=3^{-x}$ at $x=0,1,2,\ldots$ lie above the curve.
(a) Show that the limiting sum of their areas is $\dfrac{3}{2}$.
(b) Show that $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}3^{-x}dx=\dfrac{8}{9\ln3}$.
(c) Hence show that $e^{16}\lt 3^{27}$.
(a) Areas $1,\tfrac{1}{3},\tfrac{1}{9},\ldots$: GP with $a=1$, $r=\tfrac{1}{3}$.
$S_{\infty}=\dfrac{1}{1-\frac{1}{3}}=\dfrac{3}{2}$
(b) $\int_{0}^{2}3^{-x}dx=\left[-\dfrac{3^{-x}}{\ln3}\right]_{0}^{2}=-\dfrac{1}{9\ln3}+\dfrac{1}{\ln3}=\dfrac{8}{9\ln3}$
(c) The integral is below the sum of all the rectangles: $\dfrac{8}{9\ln3}\lt \dfrac{3}{2}$
$16\lt 27\ln3$, so $\ln3\gt \tfrac{16}{27}$ and $3^{27}\gt e^{16}$.
We Do 1Episode 13 · Comparing sums and integrals
The rectangles of width $1$ and height $y=5^{-x}$ at $x=0,1,2,\ldots$ lie above the curve.
(a) Show that the limiting sum of their areas is $\dfrac{5}{4}$.
(b) Show that $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}5^{-x}dx=\dfrac{24}{25\ln5}$.
(c) Hence show that $e^{96}\lt 5^{125}$.
(a) $a=1$, $r=\tfrac{1}{5}$: $S_{\infty}=\dfrac{1}{1-\frac{1}{5}}=\dfrac{5}{4}$
(b) $\left[-\dfrac{5^{-x}}{\ln5}\right]_{0}^{2}=\dfrac{1}{\ln5}-\dfrac{1}{25\ln5}=\dfrac{24}{25\ln5}$
(c) $\dfrac{24}{25\ln5}\lt \dfrac{5}{4}$, so $96\lt 125\ln5$ and $\ln5\gt \tfrac{96}{125}$. Hence $5^{125}\gt e^{96}$.

We Do 2Episode 13 · Comparing sums and integrals
Show that $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{3}2^{-x}dx=\dfrac{7}{8\ln2}$, and use the limiting sum $2$ of the rectangles under $y=2^{-x}$ to show that $e^{7}\lt 2^{16}$.
$\left[-\dfrac{2^{-x}}{\ln2}\right]_{0}^{3}=\dfrac{1}{\ln2}-\dfrac{1}{8\ln2}=\dfrac{7}{8\ln2}$
$\dfrac{7}{8\ln2}\lt 2$, so $7\lt 16\ln2$ and $\ln2\gt \tfrac{7}{16}$.
Hence $2^{16}\gt e^{7}$.
We Do 3Episode 13 · Comparing sums and integrals
Use the limiting sum of the rectangles under $y=4^{-x}$ and $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}4^{-x}dx$ to show that $e^{45}\lt 4^{64}$.
$\int_{0}^{2}4^{-x}dx=\left[-\dfrac{4^{-x}}{\ln4}\right]_{0}^{2}=\dfrac{1}{\ln4}-\dfrac{1}{16\ln4}=\dfrac{15}{16\ln4}$
Limiting sum $=\tfrac{4}{3}$.
$\dfrac{15}{16\ln4}\lt \dfrac{4}{3}$ gives $45\lt 64\ln4$, so $\ln4\gt \tfrac{45}{64}$ and $4^{64}\gt e^{45}$.

We Do 5Episode 13 · Comparing sums and integrals
Use the trapezoidal rule with one strip to show that $\ln3\lt \dfrac{4}{3}$, and explain why the estimate is too large. Hence show that $3^{3}\lt e^{4}$.
$\ln3=\int_{1}^{3}\dfrac{1}{x}dx\approx\dfrac{2}{2}\left[1+\tfrac{1}{3}\right]=\tfrac{4}{3}$
$f''(x)=\dfrac{2}{x^{3}}\gt 0$ on $[1,3]$, so the curve is concave up and the estimate is too large: $\ln3\lt \tfrac{4}{3}$.
Multiply by $3$: $3\ln3\lt 4$, so $3^{3}\lt e^{4}$.
We Do 6Episode 13 · Comparing sums and integrals
Prove that for a positive decreasing function $f$, $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{n}f(x)\,dx\lt f(0)+f(1)+\cdots+f(n-1)$.
On each interval $[k,k+1]$, $f(x)\leq f(k)$ because $f$ is decreasing.
So $\int_{k}^{k+1}f(x)\,dx\leq f(k)\times1$.
Add for $k=0,1,\ldots,n-1$: $\int_{0}^{n}f(x)\,dx\lt f(0)+\cdots+f(n-1)$ (strict because $f$ is not constant).
