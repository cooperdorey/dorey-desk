FactEpisode 1 · Differentiability
Continuity at a point
$f$ is continuous at $x=a$ when $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to a^{-}}f(x)=\lim_{x\to a^{+}}f(x)=f(a)$
$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to a}f(x)$ exists only when the limit from the left equals the limit from the right.

FactEpisode 1 · Differentiability
Differentiability at a point
$f$ is differentiable at $x=a$ if $\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\dfrac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}$ exists
that is, there is a non-vertical tangent at $P(a,\,f(a))$
I Do 2Episode 1 · Differentiability
Show that $f(x)=x^{2}-3x$ is differentiable at $x=5$.
Differentiable at $x=5$ if $\lim_{h\to 0}\dfrac{f(5+h)-f(5)}{h}$ exists.
$f(5)=5^{2}-3\times 5=10$
$f(5+h)=(5+h)^{2}-3(5+h)$
$\phantom{f(5+h)}=25+10h+h^{2}-15-3h$
$\phantom{f(5+h)}=10+7h+h^{2}$
$\dfrac{f(5+h)-f(5)}{h}=\dfrac{7h+h^{2}}{h}$
Divide by $h$, since $h\neq 0$ as it approaches $0$.
$\phantom{\dfrac{f(5+h)-f(5)}{h}}=7+h$
$\lim_{h\to 0}(7+h)=7$
The limit exists, so $f$ is differentiable at $x=5$.
There is a non-vertical tangent at $P(5,\,10)$, with gradient $7$.
FactEpisode 1 · Differentiability
Differentiable means continuous
If $f$ is differentiable at $x=a$, then $f$ is continuous at $x=a$.
Not continuous means not differentiable. The converse is false.

$\lim_{x\to 2^{+}}f(x)=\lim_{x\to 2^{+}}(x^{2}+3)=2^{2}+3=7$
$5\neq 7$, so $f$ is not continuous at $x=2$.
If $f$ is differentiable at $x=2$, then $f$ is continuous at $x=2$.
$f$ is not continuous at $x=2$, so $f$ is not differentiable at $x=2$.

Show that $f(x)=\begin{cases}x^{2}-5, & x\leq 3\\ 8x-20, & x>3\end{cases}$ is continuous but not differentiable at $x=3$.
Continuity first. If $f$ is not continuous at $x=3$, it cannot be differentiable there.
$x=3$ is in the piece $x\leq 3$.
$f(3)=3^{2}-5=4$
Limit from the left: use the piece for $x<3$.
$\lim_{x\to 3^{-}}f(x)=\lim_{x\to 3^{-}}(x^{2}-5)$
$\phantom{\lim_{x\to 3^{-}}f(x)}=3^{2}-5=4$
Limit from the right: use the piece for $x>3$. Substitute $x=3$, because $x$ only approaches $3$.
$\lim_{x\to 3^{+}}f(x)=\lim_{x\to 3^{+}}(8x-20)$
$\phantom{\lim_{x\to 3^{+}}f(x)}=8\times 3-20=4$
$\lim_{x\to 3^{-}}f(x)=\lim_{x\to 3^{+}}f(x)=f(3)$, so $f$ is continuous at $x=3$.
Gradient from the left: differentiate the piece for $x<3$.
For $x<3$, $f'(x)=2x$, so the gradient from the left is $2\times 3=6$.
Gradient from the right: differentiate the piece for $x>3$. A linear piece has a constant gradient.
For $x>3$, $f'(x)=8$, so the gradient from the right is $8$.
$6\neq 8$, so $\lim_{h\to 0}\dfrac{f(3+h)-f(3)}{h}$ does not exist.
There is no single tangent at $(3,\,4)$.
So $f$ is continuous but not differentiable at $x=3$.
