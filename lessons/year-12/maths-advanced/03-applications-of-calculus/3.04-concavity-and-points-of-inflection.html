FactEpisode 4 · Concavity and inflection
Concavity
$f''(x)>0$: concave up
$f''(x)<0$: concave down
$f''$ is the gradient of $f'$.

Let $f(x)=x^{4}+4x^{3}-7$. Find the values of $x$ for which the curve is concave down.
$f'(x)=4x^{3}+12x^{2}$
$f''(x)=12x^{2}+24x$
$\phantom{f''(x)}=12x(x+2)$
Concavity can change only where $f''(x)=0$.
$12x(x+2)=0$, so $x=0$ or $x=-2$.
Test either side of each zero.
$f''(-3)=12\times(-3)\times(-1)=36>0$
$f''(-1)=12\times(-1)\times 1=-12<0$
$f''(1)=12\times 1\times 3=36>0$
Concave down where $f''(x)<0$.
The curve is concave down for $-2
FactEpisode 4 · Concavity and inflection
Point of inflection
A point where the concavity changes
A point of inflection can occur only where $f''(x)=0$.
I Do 2Episode 4 · Concavity and inflection
Let $f(x)=x^{3}+9x^{2}-4x$. Find the coordinates of the point of inflection.
$f'(x)=3x^{2}+18x-4$
$f''(x)=6x+18$
$\phantom{f''(x)}=6(x+3)$
A point of inflection can occur only where $f''(x)=0$.
$6(x+3)=0$, so $x=-3$.
Test either side of the zero.
$f''(-4)=6\times(-1)=-6<0$
$f''(-2)=6\times 1=6>0$
The concavity changes from down to up, so there is a point of inflection.
Substitute into $f(x)$, not $f''(x)$.
$f(-3)=-27+81+12$
$\phantom{f(-3)}=66$
The point of inflection is $(-3,\,66)$.

Category · ruleEpisode 4 · Concavity and inflection
A point of inflection needs $f''(a)=0$ and a change of sign.
A squared factor does not change sign.
A cubed factor does change sign.

Find the values of $x$ for which the curve $f(x)=x^{5}-5x^{4}+2x$ is concave up, and find the coordinates of any points of inflection.
$f'(x)=5x^{4}-20x^{3}+2$
$f''(x)=20x^{3}-60x^{2}$
$\phantom{f''(x)}=20x^{2}(x-3)$
Concavity can change only where $f''(x)=0$.
$20x^{2}(x-3)=0$, so $x=0$ or $x=3$.
Test either side of each zero.
$f''(-1)=20\times 1\times(-4)=-80<0$
$f''(1)=20\times 1\times(-2)=-40<0$
$f''(4)=20\times 16\times 1=320>0$
Concave up where $f''(x)>0$.
The curve is concave up for $x>3$.
At $x=0$, $f''(x)<0$ on both sides, so the concavity does not change.
There is no point of inflection at $x=0$.
At $x=3$, $f''(x)$ changes from negative to positive, so the concavity changes.
There is a point of inflection at $x=3$.
Substitute into $f(x)$, not $f''(x)$.
$f(3)=243-405+6$
$\phantom{f(3)}=-156$
The point of inflection is $(3,\,-156)$.

We Do 2Episode 4 · Concavity and inflection
Let $f(x)=(x-5)e^{-2x}$.
(a) Show that $f''(x)=4(x-6)e^{-2x}$. (2 marks)
Product rule: $u=x-5$, $u'=1$; $v=e^{-2x}$, $v'=-2e^{-2x}$
$f'(x)=e^{-2x}-2(x-5)e^{-2x}$
$\phantom{f'(x)}=(11-2x)e^{-2x}$
Product rule again: $u=11-2x$, $u'=-2$; $v=e^{-2x}$, $v'=-2e^{-2x}$
$f''(x)=-2e^{-2x}-2(11-2x)e^{-2x}$
$\phantom{f''(x)}=(4x-24)e^{-2x}$
$\phantom{f''(x)}=4(x-6)e^{-2x}$
(b) Find the coordinates of the point of inflection, and justify that it is a point of inflection. (3 marks)
Concavity can change only where $f''(x)=0$.
$e^{-2x}>0$ for all $x$, so $f''(x)=0$ only when $x-6=0$.
$x=6$
Test either side of the zero. The sign of $f''(x)$ is the sign of $x-6$.
$f''(5)=4\times(-1)\times e^{-10}<0$
$f''(7)=4\times 1\times e^{-14}>0$
$f''(x)$ changes from negative to positive, so the concavity changes.
There is a point of inflection at $x=6$.
Substitute into $f(x)$, not $f''(x)$.
$f(6)=(6-5)e^{-12}$
$\phantom{f(6)}=e^{-12}$
The point of inflection is $(6,\,e^{-12})$.

Concave up where $\dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}>0$.
$(x^{2}+9)^{2}>0$ for all $x$, so the sign is the sign of $9-x^{2}$.
