I Do 1Episode 2 · Substitute then solve
Find the value of $b$ in the formula $m=\dfrac{a+b}{4}$ when $m=15$ and $a=9$.
Is $b$ the subject? No, $b$ is not alone on one side. Substitute, then solve.
$m=\dfrac{a+b}{4}$
Substitute $m=15$ and $a=9$. The letter to find stays.
$15=\dfrac{9+b}{4}$
The top is one group. Divided by $4$ last, so multiply both sides by $4$.
$60=9+b$
The $+9$ is done before the divide, so subtract $9$ from both sides.
$b=51$
Check: $\dfrac{9+51}{4}=\dfrac{60}{4}=15$

Find the value of $x$ in the formula $y=6x+11$ when $y=95$.
Is $x$ the subject? No. Substitute, then solve.
$y=6x+11$
$95=6x+11$
Multiplied first, then added. The $+11$ is last, so subtract $11$ from both sides.
$84=6x$
$6x$ means $6\times x$, so divide both sides by $6$.
$x=14$
We Do 2Episode 2 · Substitute then solve
Find the value of $n$ in the formula $C=30n+an+85$ when $a=12$ and $C=1\,177$.
Is $n$ the subject? No. Substitute, then solve.
$C=30n+an+85$
$1\,177=30n+12n+85$
Collect like terms. The $85$ stays separate.
$\phantom{1\,177}=42n+85$
The $+85$ is done last, so subtract $85$ from both sides.
$1\,092=42n$
Divide both sides by $42$.
$n=26$

Find the value of $r$ in the formula $M=\dfrac{p+q+r}{3}$ when $M=72$, $p=65$ and $q=80$.
Is $r$ the subject? No. Substitute, then solve.
$M=\dfrac{p+q+r}{3}$
$72=\dfrac{65+80+r}{3}$
The top is one group. Divided by $3$ last, so multiply both sides by $3$.
$216=65+80+r$
Add the numbers on the right.
$\phantom{216}=145+r$
Subtract $145$ from both sides.
$r=71$
We Do 4Episode 2 · Substitute then solve
The cost, $C$ dollars, is given by $C=40n+an+75$.
(a) Find the value of $C$ when $a=15$ and $n=12$. (1 mark)
Is $C$ the subject? Yes. Substitute and evaluate.
$C=40n+an+75$
$C=40\times 12+15\times 12+75$
$\phantom{C}=480+180+75$
$\phantom{C}=735$
(b) Find the value of $n$ when $a=15$ and $C=1\,120$. (2 marks)
Is $n$ the subject? No. Substitute, then solve.
$1\,120=40n+15n+75$
Collect like terms. The $75$ stays separate.
$\phantom{1\,120}=55n+75$
The $+75$ is done last, so subtract $75$ from both sides.
$1\,045=55n$
Divide both sides by $55$.
$n=19$

We Do 5Episode 2 · Substitute then solve
Find the value of $x$ in the formula $T=x+\dfrac{x-k}{3}$ when $k=5$ and $T=21$.
Is $x$ the subject? No. Substitute, then solve.
$T=x+\dfrac{x-k}{3}$
$21=x+\dfrac{x-5}{3}$
Multiply every term by $3$.
$63=3x+(x-5)$
Remove the brackets, then collect like terms.
$\phantom{63}=3x+x-5$
$\phantom{63}=4x-5$
The $-5$ is done last, so add $5$ to both sides.
$68=4x$
Divide both sides by $4$.
$x=17$
